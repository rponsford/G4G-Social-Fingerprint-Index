More to look for in a 990

  • Consistent reporting: are the same definitions and accounting methods used each year?
  • Statement of Functional Expenses (Part IX): the clearest view of how money is spent.
  • Board compensation (Part VII): is the board paid? If so, is it appropriate?
  • Highest-paid employees: are salaries in line with similar-sized nonprofits?
  • Unrelated business income: is income coming from activities outside the mission? It can affect tax status or raise questions.
  • Related organizations and transactions (Schedule R): any conflicts of interest or dealings with insiders?
  • Schedule O (supplemental information): often where the organization explains anomalies or strategy.
Step 5 · Exploring the heart of the organization

Reaching out to the organization

Numbers matter, but culture carries the mission. To understand the intangibles of a nonprofit, nothing beats a real conversation. Whether by phone, video, or in person, speaking directly with nonprofit leaders helps you understand their heart, their culture, and their capacity to follow through.

Review the suggested questions below and choose the ones that resonate most with you or your group. Add your own, including any that came up while you reviewed their 990. Together they'll help you assess leadership, transparency, impact, and values.

Time guide: 30–45 minutes

Questions from your numbers

Based on the ratios above that may warrant a closer look.

    17 questions to ask a nonprofit representative
    Mission and story
    1. How would you describe the mission and purpose of your organization?
    2. What services does your organization provide, and to whom?
    3. How did it get started and how long has it been in existence?
    4. How long have you been associated with the organization?
    5. Why are you personally a part of the organization?
    Finances and future
    1. What are the primary revenue sources for the organization?
    2. Where do you expect the organization to be financially in 5 years?
    3. If a generous donor offered you $1,000,000, how would you use it?
    Leadership and people
    1. How many people are on the board of directors and how are they chosen?
    2. What are the requirements to be on the board of directors?
    3. How many paid employees does the organization have?
    4. How many volunteers does the organization have?
    5. What are some of the greatest needs that you have as an organization?
    6. What percentage of volunteers also donate financially?
    Heart and culture
    1. What do you enjoy most about being a part of this organization?
    2. If you could change one thing about this organization, what would that be?
    3. Is there anything else you'd like me to know about your organization?

    Final thoughts for donors

    Use these numbers to inform your conversation with the organization, not to replace it. They should raise questions and point you to trends, anomalies, strengths, and concerns. Pair your findings with:

    • A conversation with the nonprofit
    • Their impact reports
    • A visit to a program or event
    • Questions about mission, leadership, and goals

    An effective nonprofit might not have "perfect" ratios, but it should have a clear story that explains its numbers, decisions, and outcomes.
